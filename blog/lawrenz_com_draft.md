**Title: Introducing Stratum-FFHQ: Giving Back to the Generative AI Community**

When Tero Karras, Samuli Laine, and Timo Aila at NVIDIA released the Flickr-Faces-HQ (FFHQ) dataset in 2019 alongside their groundbreaking StyleGAN architecture, it permanently altered the trajectory of generative AI. Consisting of 70,000 high-quality, perfectly aligned faces, FFHQ became the undisputed gold standard for human face synthesis. It didn't just train StyleGAN; it served as the foundational bedrock for countless transformative research projects over the last half-decade—from facial restoration frameworks like GFP-GAN to text-driven semantic manipulation tools like StyleCLIP and ALAE.

Today, however, the paradigm of generative AI has shifted. Training state-of-the-art architectures, like large-scale Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and highly controllable generative models, requires much more than just high-quality pixels. They require dense, multi-modal conditioning: granular semantic embeddings, precise spatial maps, and highly descriptive text.

To give back to the vibrant research community that was built on top of FFHQ, I am officially releasing **stratum-ffhq** as free, public training data. 

**Modernizing a Foundational Dataset**
`stratum-ffhq` is a deep enrichment layer for the original 70,000 FFHQ images, processed locally using the `stratum-hq` pipeline. Instead of having to spend thousands of GPU hours extracting conditioning data, researchers can now download this dataset to instantly access:
*   **Semantic Foundations:** Global and per-patch DINOv3 ViT-L/16 embeddings for deep spatial understanding.
*   **Text & Language:** Dense, objective captions generated by Gemma 3 27B, packaged with T5-Large hidden states and attention masks.
*   **Geometry & Mechanics:** 28-class body-part segmentation, depth estimation, and surface normals via Sapiens-1B, alongside 133-point COCO-WholeBody kinematics via DWPose.

**Decoupling Representation from Pixels**
A core philosophy behind this release is respecting the provenance and licensing of the original dataset. To strictly avoid licensing conflicts and honor the original creators' rights, **we do not republish the actual pixel values of the FFHQ images.** 

By decoupling the learned representations from the raw pixels, `stratum-ffhq` provides a safe, open-weights-compatible metadata layer. You simply download the original pixel data directly from NVIDIA's official repository, comply with their CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license, and merge it with our pre-computed tensors using the `image_id`. 

The generative community gave us the tools to synthesize reality. With `stratum-ffhq`, I hope to provide the open-source community with the data needed to control it.
